# Verification pass, 2026-10-02

Every row below was read from the provider's own page on 2026-10-02 and
compared with the value the catalogue uses. `sources/2026-10-02/MANIFEST.tsv`
pins the URL, the HTTP status, the byte count and the SHA-256 of the copy read.
Raw HTML is not committed: it is somebody else's page. Re-fetch with
`sh tools/fetch-sources.sh`.

The first round of this catalogue priced always-on presets named `nano-box`,
`agent-box`, `interp` and `devbox`; the rows below still use those names
because that is what was verified against those presets. The current
catalogue re-prices the same provider facts over six usage horizons
(`data/usage.md`), so a dollar figure here and a dollar figure there can
differ by the billing duration, not by a changed rate.

Currency conversion, where used: 1.1378 USD/EUR, the ECB rate 2026-09-28 that
the battleships corpus itself uses. Stated here because a different rate moves
the European rows by a few percent.

## scaleway-stardust — present in the corpus, and correctly priced (self-correction)

- Source: `https://www.scaleway.com/en/pricing/virtual-instances/` (200, 6,782,563 bytes).
- Quote (rendered table): `STARDUST1-S | 1 vCPU | 1 GB | 100 Mbps | €0.0006 / HOUR`.
- Quote (footer): `Storage (local, block) and attached public IPv4 addresses are excluded.`
- Quote (embedded catalogue JSON): `"apiId":"STARDUST1-S","catalogId":"p_instance_stardust-s","hardware":{...,"cpu":{"arch":"x64","sharedCpu":true,..."virtual":{"count":1}}...,"storages":[{"count":1,"dynamicLocal":{"maxSize":10000000000,"type":"SSD"}}...]}` and `"price":{"hourly":{"sku":"/instance/server/stardust1_s/fr-par-1","value":{"currencyCode":"EUR","nanos":600000,"units":0}}...}`.
- Arithmetic: €0.0006/h × 1.1378 = $0.00068268/h; × 730 h = **$0.498/mo compute**, plus
the requested disk at Scaleway's block-storage rate because the page excludes
storage. The corpus card already encodes this (`stardust` mode, size
`STARDUST1-S`, `hour: 0.00068268`, `disk_gib: 0`), so `nano-box` prices it at
**$1.58**, not $0.50, and it is #2.
- **Self-correction.** The first draft of this pass read `grep -ril stardust`
as a hit on the filename only, inspected the first three Scaleway modes, and
concluded the tier was missing. It then added a duplicate card
(`research/cards-extra/scaleway-stardust.json`) that assumed 10 GiB of included
disk and ranked Stardust #1 at $0.498. The card already carried the tier and
the page excludes storage, so the duplicate was wrong on both counts; it was
deleted before publication and the ranking regenerated.
- Verdict: **confirms the corpus**. The named edge case is covered; there is no
  missing-tier finding here. Another pass should still check whether the
  `dynamicLocal` SSD is billed at the block-storage rate or a cheaper local
  rate, which is the one number in this row that is a modelling choice.

## netcup VPS — RESOLVED: the card's `term-1` figure is 15 % high

- Sources: `https://www.netcup.com/en/server/vps` (200) and
  `https://www.netcup.com/en/server/vps-lite` (200), re-fetched 2026-10-02.
  The re-fetched copies are pinned in `sources/2026-10-02v2/MANIFEST.tsv`;
  a second fetch of each page returned the same byte count and content, so the
  figures below are stable, not a one-off render.
- **What revision 1 got wrong.** Revision 1 quoted only the *first* Lite price it
  found (`€5.86`) against the card's `$5.029` and called a 7–11 % gap that runs
  the wrong way. The page carries **five** Lite plans in order (pico, nano,
  Lite 1, Lite 2, Lite 3, Lite 4) at `€2.21 / €3.69 / €5.86 / €9.50 / €16.66 /
  €30.86`, on minimum terms of `12 / 6 / 6 / 3 / 2 / 1` months. Reading the
  card against the wrong row of that table produced the revision-1 "dispute".
- **Correct pairing, EUR net = shown price / 1.19, USD = net x 1.1378:**

  | plan | min term | shown (incl. VAT) | net ×1.1378 | card | delta |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | VPS Lite 1 G12.5s | 6 M | €5.86 | $5.602 | $5.029 | card 10 % low |
  | VPS Lite 2 G12.5s | 3 M | €9.50 | $9.081 | $8.511 | card 6 % low |
  | VPS Lite 3 G12.5s | 2 M | €16.66 | $15.928 | $15.360 | card 4 % low |
  | VPS Lite 4 G12.5s | 1 M | €30.86 | $29.504 | $28.934 | card 2 % low |

  The gap closes as the term shortens, which identifies the cause: the card
  encodes each Lite plan at its **1-month** rate and ignores the longer minimum
  term actually attached to the small sizes. It is the same shape as the
  `term-1` / `term-12` / `term-24` ladder — term-1 is the dearest — so the card
  picks the dearest term for every Lite size.
- **Bigger, separate error.** "No preference Europe" (the card's default)
  selects `term-1` = VPS 500 G12.5 at **$8.511**, not `$5.029`. So the entry row
  this catalogue publishes is a different plan: `$5.029` is *VPS Lite 1 G12.5s*,
  a 6-month-minimum Lite plan, not the standard VPS 500 G12.5 on the page shown
  for the "No preference Europe" location.
- **Cross-check.** VPS 500 G12.5 page €8.26 incl. VAT = €6.941 net = $7.898,
  against the card's `term-12` $7.327 (7 % low) and page baseline €9.50 =
  $7.983 net = $9.083 against card `term-1` $8.511 (6 % low). Two independent
  term columns read the same way, so this is not one misread price.
- **Verdict.** Confirmed as a **lower bound**, now with the mechanism: netcup's
  entry price for the comparison shape is **$8.51/mo on a 1-month term** or
  **$7.33/mo on a 12-month term**, not $5.03. All three figures exclude the
  €0.504 net IPv4 (the card's `ipv4_month` is $0.574). netcup stays cheap on
  the 24/7 column but is no longer the entry-priced provider there.

## Oracle Cloud — cannot be verified from here, and Always Free is not modelled

- **The block is the whole domain, not one page.** `https://www.oracle.com/`,
  `/cloud/compute/pricing/`, `/cloud/price-list/`, `/cloud/calculator/`,
  `/cloud/free/`, `docs.oracle.com`, `docs.cloud.oracle.com` and the
  `apexapps.oracle.com` price-list API all return the **same 1,339-byte 403**
  from this host. The body is not a bot wall with a challenge; it is an
  export-control refusal:

  > `403 Oracle Export Error In compliance with U.S. and applicable Export laws
  > we are unable to process your request ... Reference ID: 0.cf9c7a5c...`

  So "pricing page returns 403" understates it: **no first-party Oracle URL is
  reachable from this sandbox, including the reference documentation.** The row
  is carried from the corpus card and marked unverified. Every mode and the
  `$300` one-time credit are as the card has them.
- **The card's own numbers are internally consistent and cited.** It quotes the
  burstable page ("charged according to the baseline OCPU ... regardless of
  whether the actual CPU utilization is at the baseline, below the baseline, or
  bursts above") and the Arm/pricing pages, all dated 2026-09-28. The comparison
  shape prices on `E4 12.5% baseline burstable` at $3.09 for 10 h/day x30 and
  $7.41 for 24/7.
- **Always Free is a resource allowance, and the engine does not model it.**
  The card records, from
  `docs.oracle.com/.../FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm`:

  > All tenancies get the first **1,500 OCPU hours and 9,000 GB hours per
  > month** for free on `VM.Standard.A1.Flex`
  > = 1,500 x $0.01 + 9,000 x $0.0015 = **$28.50/month** of A1 compute.

  The comparison shape is **2 vCPU / 4 GiB**, and A1 OCPU = 1 core. At the
  realistic month it uses **600 OCPU-h and 1,200 GiB-h**; at 24/7 it uses
  **1,440 OCPU-h and 2,880 GiB-h**. Both are inside the 1,500 / 9,000 allowance,
  so **a qualifying account pays $0 for this shape even running 24/7**, against
  the $7.41 the paid column shows. This is the largest single understatement of
  cost in the catalogue.
- **There is a first-party conflict about the size of that allowance**, recorded
  in the card and not resolved here: the Always Free page currently says
  1,500 OCPU-h / 9,000 GB-h (**2 cores / 12 GiB**), while the Arm and pricing
  pages retain 3,000 / 18,000 (**4 cores / 24 GiB**). The card refuses to choose
  ("No blanket guarantee of the larger tier"). At this shape it does not matter:
  both readings cover 24/7.
- **Why it is not simply ranked at $0.** The allowance is scoped to the A1
  regime only (it cannot pay for x86 compute), home-region capacity can be
  exhausted and idle resources reclaimed, and the engine prices a resource
  allowance as list. Ranking Oracle at $0 would claim a guarantee the vendor
  does not give. The row keeps the paid rate and says the allowance is not
  modelled; the number to act on for a qualifying account is $0.
